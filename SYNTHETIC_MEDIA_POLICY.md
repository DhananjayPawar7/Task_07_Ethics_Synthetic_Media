# Synthetic Media Creation, Representation, and Publication Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs the creation, editing, approval, publication, and retention of synthetic audio, video, and likeness-based media by a University Athletics Communications Office.

The policy is designed to permit limited useful applications of synthetic media while protecting accurate attribution, individual consent, audience trust, institutional accountability, and the ability to identify approved content after publication.

This policy applies to employees, student workers, interns, contractors, vendors, and any other person creating or publishing content on behalf of the Athletics Communications Office.

---

## 2. Definitions

### Synthetic media
Audio, video, or imagery generated or materially altered by an automated system so that speech, appearance, movement, or performance is presented in a form that was not directly recorded that way.

### Identifiable person
Any athlete, coach, recruit, staff member, alumnus, donor, broadcaster, university employee, or other individual whose identity could reasonably be recognized through voice, likeness, name, or distinctive characteristics.

### Synthetic representation
Synthetic media that could reasonably be interpreted as depicting or speaking for an identifiable person.

### Generic synthetic narration
Synthetic speech using a voice that is not presented as, or designed to imitate, a real identifiable person.

### High-consequence communication
A communication involving emergency instructions, safety, health or injury status, discipline, eligibility, recruiting commitments, employment decisions, legal matters, crisis response, or another subject where the identity and accountability of the human speaker are material to the audience.

---

## 3. Permitted Uses

The office may use **generic synthetic media that does not imitate a real identifiable person** for the following purposes, subject to disclosure and review requirements:

1. generic narration for internal training or instructional materials;
2. accessibility prototypes and draft narration used to evaluate script length, pacing, or format;
3. clearly labeled research or educational demonstrations of synthetic-media technology;
4. temporary production mockups that will be replaced by a human recording before external publication;
5. low-risk public informational narration where the synthetic nature is clearly disclosed and the content does not create the impression that a real person personally delivered the message;
6. fully synthetic characters used for clearly fictional or educational purposes where no reasonable audience would interpret the character as a real university representative.

Permitted status does not remove the requirement for factual review, disclosure, provenance records, and approval.

---

## 4. Prohibited Uses

The Athletics Communications Office must not create or publish synthetic media that:

1. impersonates an identifiable person without documented consent;
2. depicts an athlete, coach, recruit, staff member, alumnus, donor, journalist, public official, or other identifiable person saying or doing something they did not actually say or do, except for a clearly fictionalized and explicitly consented production that has received enhanced review;
3. fabricates quotations, press-conference answers, interviews, endorsements, recruiting commitments, disciplinary statements, eligibility decisions, injury or health updates, emergency instructions, or crisis communications;
4. is designed to cause an audience to believe synthetic media is an authentic recording;
5. intentionally removes, conceals, or weakens a required synthetic-media disclosure;
6. uses synthetic representation to shame, threaten, harass, sexualize, ridicule, or exploit a person;
7. uses synthetic media to create false evidence of an event, meeting, statement, or action;
8. is automatically published at scale without the review controls required by this policy;
9. relies on a vendor or workflow that prevents the office from retaining the required creation and approval records;
10. presents a synthetic voice as an official human spokesperson for a high-consequence communication.

**A disclosure does not convert a prohibited use into a permitted use.**

---

## 5. Consent Workflow for Identifiable People

Synthetic representation of an identifiable person may not begin until the office has documented consent for the specific project.

### 5.1 Who obtains consent

The project owner is responsible for obtaining consent. The Athletics Communications Director or designee must verify that the consent is complete before generation begins.

### 5.2 What the consent must specify

The record must identify:

- the person whose identity will be synthesized;
- whether voice, likeness, or both will be generated;
- the purpose of the project;
- the intended audience;
- planned distribution channels;
- the approved script or defined subject boundaries;
- whether editing or excerpting is permitted;
- whether reuse in future projects is permitted;
- the start and expiration date of permission;
- the process for withdrawing permission for future use.

### 5.3 What does not count as consent

The following do not automatically authorize synthetic representation:

- employment by the university;
- membership on a team;
- participation in ordinary photography or video;
- a general publicity release that does not explicitly cover synthetic representation;
- previous participation in another synthetic-media project;
- verbal assumptions that the person “would probably be fine with it.”

### 5.4 Revocation

If consent is withdrawn, no new synthetic media using that person's identity may be created or published after the effective date of revocation. Existing media controlled by the office must be reviewed for removal or continued retention according to the terms of the original consent and any separate institutional record-retention requirement.

---

## 6. Disclosure Standards

Every approved synthetic-media artifact must be identifiable as synthetic without requiring the audience to investigate.

### Audio

- Include a spoken disclosure at or near the beginning.
- Include `AI_GENERATED` or `SYNTHETIC` in the working and archival filename.
- Include a synthetic-media disclosure in the accompanying post, page, or description.

### Video

- Include an on-screen disclosure at the beginning.
- Maintain a persistent visual indicator when feasible for content that could otherwise be mistaken for an authentic recording.
- Include a disclosure in captions or accompanying publication text.

### Excerpts

Any excerpt intended to circulate independently must carry its own disclosure. Staff may not assume that a disclosure contained only at the beginning of a longer original will remain attached after clipping.

### Internal drafts

Internal synthetic drafts must remain labeled so they cannot be mistaken for authentic recordings if forwarded or downloaded.

If the intended distribution format cannot preserve an adequate disclosure, the office must not use synthetic representation in that format.

---

## 7. Provenance Requirements

For every synthetic-media project approved for publication, the office must maintain an internal provenance record containing:

- the final approved file;
- a cryptographic hash of the final approved file where practical;
- creation date;
- project owner;
- generation tool and model/version where available;
- source script or prompt;
- relevant generation settings where available;
- consent record when an identifiable person is involved;
- approval record;
- publication channels and URLs/locations where applicable;
- known edited or excerpted versions.

If the generation or editing tool supports C2PA content credentials, watermarking, or comparable provenance metadata, those features should remain enabled unless the approver documents a technical reason why they cannot be used.

The office must retain the original generated export alongside the publication copy when the two differ.

This requirement exists because metadata may not survive ordinary transformations. Task 6 demonstrated that C2PA markers present in an original synthetic-audio export could disappear after re-encoding.

---

## 8. Review and Approval

### 8.1 Low-risk generic synthetic media

Before publication, a staff member other than the creator must review the final artifact for:

- factual accuracy;
- clarity of disclosure;
- misleading attribution;
- compliance with this policy;
- obvious generation errors;
- consistency with the approved script and purpose.

### 8.2 Synthetic representation of an identifiable person

Publication requires:

1. documented consent;
2. review by the Athletics Communications Director or designee;
3. confirmation that the script and final output remain inside the scope of consent;
4. verification of all required disclosures;
5. completion of the provenance record;
6. additional institutional review when the content touches a high-consequence subject.

### 8.3 Ambiguity rule

If reviewers disagree about whether a reasonable audience could mistake the output for an authentic recording of a real person, publication must stop until the ambiguity is resolved.

When doubt remains, use a real human recording or a clearly generic synthetic narrator instead.

---

## 9. Automation and Scale

Synthetic-media generation may not be connected directly to automated public publishing without a separately approved workflow.

Batch generation is permitted only when:

- the use category is permitted under this policy;
- the content template has been approved;
- required disclosures are automatically included and verified;
- data fields come from an approved and validated source;
- no unauthorized identifiable-person representation is introduced;
- a documented human review procedure is used;
- the office can identify all outputs in a batch;
- the office can immediately stop publication when an error is detected.

The ability to generate hundreds of artifacts does not justify reducing review simply because generation itself is inexpensive.

---

## 10. Detection and Verification

Automated AI-media detectors may be used during investigation or incident response as a **secondary signal**.

A detector result must not be treated as final proof of authenticity or synthetic origin by itself.

The office's primary method for verifying media it created must be its own provenance and approval records. Detection is most useful when the source of a suspicious artifact is unknown.

---

## 11. Incident Response

If synthetic media produced by, attributed to, or appearing to represent the Athletics Communications Office is misused, misattributed, stripped of disclosure, altered, or distributed to an unintended audience, staff must:

1. preserve the approved original and its provenance record;
2. notify the Athletics Communications Director immediately;
3. identify known versions and distribution channels;
4. compare the circulating artifact with the approved original;
5. issue a clarification or correction when audience confusion is plausible;
6. request relabeling, correction, or removal from channels the office can influence;
7. notify any represented individual whose identity is involved;
8. preserve relevant screenshots, URLs, timestamps, and files;
9. document the response and lessons learned;
10. review whether the affected project type, tool, or distribution channel should be suspended.

Suspected fraud, threats, malicious impersonation, or another high-consequence incident must be escalated through the university's appropriate institutional process rather than handled as an ordinary communications correction.

---

## 12. Refusal Conditions

The office will decline to create synthetic media when:

- valid consent cannot be obtained or verified;
- the audience is likely to mistake synthetic representation for an authentic recording;
- the request involves a high-consequence statement that should come from a real accountable speaker;
- required disclosure cannot reasonably remain attached to the content;
- required provenance records cannot be retained;
- the project's purpose depends on hiding how the content was created;
- the proposed scale makes meaningful review impossible;
- reviewers cannot resolve a reasonable concern about deception, dignity, attribution, or consent;
- the requested use would remain harmful or misleading even if prominently labeled synthetic.

When one of these conditions applies, the office should use an alternative such as written text, a real human recording, a generic narrator, or no media artifact at all.

---

## 13. Training and Audit

Staff who create or approve synthetic media must receive training on this policy before publishing such content.

The Athletics Communications Director should periodically review a sample of projects for:

- consent documentation;
- disclosure compliance;
- provenance records;
- approval history;
- tool/version documentation;
- incident patterns;
- batch-generation controls.

A recurring failure in one tool, workflow, or distribution channel is grounds for temporary suspension until the process is reviewed.

---

## 14. Policy Review

This policy should be reviewed whenever:

- the office adopts a substantially new synthetic-media capability;
- a significant incident occurs;
- a recurring disclosure or provenance failure is identified;
- changes in institutional requirements make the existing process inadequate.

The goal of review is not to expand synthetic-media use automatically. It is to confirm that any continued use remains compatible with consent, attribution, accountability, and audience trust.
